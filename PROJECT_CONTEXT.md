# TINELLA — Canonical Project Context

## Objective
Develop an original, manufacturable collection of CNC stone bowls, vases, trays, lamps, seating and tables with Marmi Cave Tinella in Puglia. The design language is architectural mass, void, proportion, controlled light and honest material. Sources of inspiration include Manuel Aires Mateus and Christiane Perrochon, but do not reproduce their protected designs.

## Stone and manufacturing
Tinella's catalogue and workshop capabilities must be checked with the company. Candidate local stones from earlier research: Biancone, Fiorito Adriatico, Filetto Rosso, Chianca. Do not assume strength, density, porosity, frost resistance, tooling or finishes without samples and testing. Prioritize offcuts and parts that fit existing CNC machines.

## Canonical object names
VUOTO: square/rectangular bowl with carefully displaced excavation.
SOGLIA: narrow stone vase with concealed removable watertight insert.
TORRE: vertical cylindrical vase with machined opening.
PIEGA: sculptural shallow asymmetric folded bowl/tray.
SEDUTA: architectural stone chair, ergonomics and structural engineering required.
ARCO: small table/stool with **two arches rotated 45° in the top section**. Previous front/side views were inconsistent; rebuild in parametric CAD.
MASSA: architectural dining table; intersecting support geometries.
BLOCCO: stone bench with deliberate modular proportions.
BAMBOO: one-seat chair and three-seat version based on the user's photographs: marble cylindrical tubes and bamboo-node detail, no showroom ropes, **no wheels**, cylindrical feet matching stone frame, soft removable cushions in **C&C Milano MAREMMA** fabric (article/color still to confirm). Cushions only in renders; technical drawings show clear cushion zones and underlying seat/back supports.

## Previous iteration history
Iterations 01–05 introduced imagery and flat drawings, but several renderings and orthographic views conflicted, particularly SOGLIA, SEDUTA, ARCO and MASSA. These are *visual brainstorming*, not master geometry. Later flat-only sketches still require CAD reconciliation; do not claim approval or manufacturing readiness.

## New workflow: geometry first
1. Freeze requirement for one object.
2. Create actual parametric 3D CAD, with millimetre units and named components.
3. Project **plan, front, side, sections** from that exact 3D solid.
4. Check volumes, interference, wall thickness, tool access and support loads.
5. Export STEP and a mesh derived from identical geometry.
6. Make Blender rendering from the exported mesh, not a newly invented shape.
7. Store source code/model, STEP, drawings, render and revision together.
8. Obtain actual quarry material and engineer sign-off before manufacturing.

## Mathematical design rules
φ = (1+√5)/2; Fibonacci sequences; √2 rectangles; primes as discrete part counts. Treat proportional systems as generative tools, not universal historical facts. Some popular claims about golden ratio in ancient monuments are contested.

## Material versus aesthetic
Only documented natural quarried stone belongs in the stone-only competitor set. Ceramic gres, porcelain, cast stone, synthetic composite and marble-look finishes are separate. Mark hybrids separately.

## Market strategy
First validate four small objects, then move into higher-ticket furniture, lighting and BAMBOO after structural prototyping. Record evidence types rather than inventing confidential sales numbers.

## Current design preference
The user specifically approved the warm-toned **TINELLA — Stone Object Collection** editorial overview depicting VUOTO / SOGLIA / TORRE / PIEGA / BAMBOO / SEDUTA / ARCO / MASSA / BLOCCO. It is a visual direction, **not CAD**. Preserve restrained limestone and the recognisable bamboo tubular chair.

## Open technical questions
Stone batches/colourways, machine axis and envelope, hollowing tolerance, fixture design, transport weights, abrasion/waterproofing, lamp certification, furniture structural testing, BAMBOO joint stress, precise MAREMMA SKU and upholstery usage.
